Why it mattered

The profession changed faster than its degrees

This page documents the impact of the curriculum changes the School of Accountancy made between 2017 and 2025, when it rebuilt its accounting degrees so that every graduate leaves with data analytics skills and a working grasp of sustainability reporting. It sets out why those changes were needed, what the School did, and what the evidence shows for students, employers, the profession and accounting education more broadly.

The work of an accountant changed more in the past decade than in the several before it. Reconciliation, journal preparation and audit sampling were progressively automated; what grew was work with large and unstructured datasets, the systems and controls that generate them, and the measurement and reporting of non-financial information. Yet the standard accounting degree, in Singapore and elsewhere, still treated analytics as a business-school elective and sustainability as someone else's subject.

The question for any accounting school: does its degree still prepare graduates for the profession they are entering?
A curriculum transformed

Analytics for every graduate, sustainability in every core course

Rather than offering analytics and sustainability as electives for interested students, the School made analytics compulsory for every accounting undergraduate and embedded sustainability across the core accounting curriculum. Each change began with structured consultation with industry leaders, after which the faculty teaching and researching in those areas shaped proposals through the School's curriculum committee, its Advisory Board and, from 2020, a dedicated Data Analytics Advisory Board.

The changes were introduced before the relevant regulations were announced, or around the same time, rather than after new requirements took effect. The 2018 analytics launches came three years before the first comparable programme at another Singapore university; comparable programmes followed at NTU (2021, 2022) and NUS (2022), and the 2022 sustainability elective was followed by NTU and NUS offerings in 2024.

The curriculum did not draw on external expertise alone. Faculty research on detecting financial misreporting, measuring audit-report informativeness with machine learning, generative AI and investor trading, and Scope 3 supply-chain emissions disclosures is taught from directly in the statistical programming, forensic analytics, data modelling, capstone and sustainability courses.

Who benefited

Four groups, one redesigned degree

Students and graduates

Every undergraduate since 2019 has completed statistical programming. The ADA second major has become the most common second major among accounting graduates, taken by 22–36% of each cohort since 2020. Across five Graduate Employment Surveys, 56–79% of each ADA cohort joined a Big Four firm, and ADA graduates entered data analyst, data scientist and systems analyst roles that did not exist for accounting graduates a decade ago. ACCT431 Sustainability Accounting and Reporting has drawn 221 enrolments since 2022 from accounting, business, economics and law.

Employers

Big Four firms publicly endorsed the 2018 launches as preparing graduates for "the finance function of the future". Deloitte Southeast Asia entered a "first-of-kind partnership" to build audit analytics into the programme; senior practitioners from the Big Four, banks and regulators sit on the Data Analytics Advisory Board; and firms sponsor and host the capstone consulting projects through which students apply analytics to live business problems.

Professional bodies and regulators

Two ISCA sustainability studies co-authored by faculty — on sustainability jobs and skills (2022) and on sustainability transformation in manufacturing (2023) — reached a membership of more than 40,000. Faculty serve on the ISCA Council, the Singapore CA Qualification committees, ISCA's Sustainability and Climate Change Committee and AI Taskforce, MAS's Green Finance Working Group and the ASSB advisory committee. The School is SGX's research partner on governance disclosures across all listed companies.

Accounting education more broadly

Comparable programmes followed at NTU and NUS. The pedagogy behind the redesign won the inaugural European Accounting Association Accounting Education Excellence Award (2022), first prize in the CAAA Howard Teall Innovation in Accounting Education Award (2023) and QS-Wharton Reimagine Education awards (2021, 2022). In 2023 a faculty member became the first academic in Singapore to receive the American Accounting Association's Outstanding Accounting Educator Award.

What made it possible

Four choices behind a decade of reform

  1. Compulsion over choice. Most programmes responded to the analytics shift by adding electives, which reach the students who already see the need. Making statistical programming compulsory, and embedding sustainability in every core module, meant the redesign reached every graduate — and every employer of those graduates.

  2. Research linked to teaching. The faculty studying analytics and sustainability reporting were the same people redesigning the modules, so the curriculum kept pace with the field. SMU's recognition of industry-facing and pedagogical research alongside academic publication made this possible.

  3. Timing built on relationships. Years of jointly organised conferences and industry projects with the Big Four, banks, professional bodies and regulators gave the School early sight of the capabilities that would later become requirements.

  4. A school-wide effort. No single faculty member could have made analytics compulsory or embedded sustainability across the core. Curriculum design, done deliberately and at scale, is a form of impact in its own right.

What comes next

Generative and agentic AI are now reshaping accounting work, automating not only routine tasks but parts of the analysis, drafting and judgement that the redesigned curriculum prepared graduates for. Between 2017 and 2025 the School showed it could anticipate changes in the profession and respond through school-wide curriculum reform. The challenge now is to apply that same approach to AI — preparing graduates for emerging demands before they become established professional requirements.
